# Main heroes

## H1 — Public-facing lead hero

This character is the emotional entry point but is not an all-powerful flying paragon.

### H1-A — Goldseam / Amara Saye

- **Civilian work:** conservator repairing mosaics, theatres and public monuments.
- **Power:** sees stress and past impacts trapped in manufactured surfaces. She can draw the damage into glowing seams, stabilising one structure while storing the displaced force in ceramic tokens.
- **Cost:** overloaded tokens crack unpredictably; she must discharge them into safe foundations.
- **Combat identity:** support, barriers made from existing architecture, controlled knockback.
- **Look:** cream work jacket, oxblood trousers, irregular glazed plates and visible amber repair lines; uncovered face; no cape or chest emblem.
- **Personality:** exacting, compassionate, sceptical of spectacle.
- **Story hook:** she saved a landmark during the city disaster but failed to inspect the illegal homes beneath it.

### H1-B — Beaconry / Noor Baptiste

- **Civilian work:** emergency radio producer and missing-person coordinator.
- **Power:** connections between people appear to her as luminous routes. With consent from one endpoint, she can turn a route into a beacon, message path or rescue tether.
- **Cost:** she temporarily experiences the strongest emotion travelling along each route.
- **Combat identity:** team coordination, civilian location, ally recovery and enemy isolation—little direct damage.
- **Look:** coral radio coat, enamel headset fins positioned behind rather than over the ears, ribbon aerials and a hand-cranked signal lantern.
- **Personality:** charismatic in public, emotionally crowded in private.
- **Story hook:** a beacon can reveal where someone is, but never whether they want to be found.

### H1-C — Kindling House / Owen Rhee

- **Civilian work:** social-housing architect.
- **Power:** rooms where people have safely sheltered leave “warm plans” in his memory. He can unfold one remembered room around nearby people for a few seconds: a kitchen that calms panic, a stairwell that redirects movement, or a bedroom that muffles impact.
- **Cost:** every projection fades one detail from his memory of the original home.
- **Combat identity:** area control and emotional protection.
- **Look:** fitted teal survey suit, folding brass ruler, translucent floor-plan panels and warm window-shaped light; no armour.
- **Personality:** optimistic, politically stubborn, privately afraid of forgetting his childhood.
- **Story hook:** who owns the memory of a home after that home is demolished?

## H2 — Investigator/controller

### H2-A — Sidechannel / Dev Malik

- **Civilian work:** accessibility-focused acoustic engineer.
- **Power:** hears the hidden route by which a force travelled and can send the next matching force along that route instead.
- **Cost:** each reroute removes one frequency from his hearing until he rests.
- **Combat identity:** counters, ricochets, traps and forensic reconstruction.
- **Look:** petrol-green wrap coat, chalk cuffs, bone-conduction tabs and air-current streamers.
- **Personality:** dry, patient and uncomfortable with command.
- **Mystery hook:** he can reconstruct how a window broke, but not why the person threw the object.

### H2-B — Blind Index / Inez Kaur

- **Civilian work:** archivist for uncatalogued municipal collections.
- **Power:** by closing her eyes, Inez can perceive everything in a room that has been deliberately omitted from a list, map, photograph or report.
- **Cost:** while using the power she cannot perceive anything properly documented—including allies whose identities are public.
- **Combat identity:** concealment detection, evidence discovery and selective invisibility.
- **Look:** saffron tabard covered in blank index tabs, dark safety lenses and white gloves stained with coloured filing ink.
- **Personality:** curious, mischievous and furious about institutional erasure.
- **Mystery hook:** someone has removed an entire neighbourhood from every official record.

### H2-C — Afterimage Clerk / Mateo Orr

- **Civilian work:** insurance loss assessor.
- **Power:** sees the last three intended uses of a damaged object and can make one intention briefly happen again—a door tries to close, a lift tries to rise, a weapon tries to return to its case.
- **Cost:** the object is permanently less capable afterward.
- **Combat identity:** environmental puzzle solving and disarming.
- **Look:** plum waistcoat, rolled sleeves, glass loupe and floating carbon-copy gestures in cyan and amber.
- **Personality:** courteous, anxious and unexpectedly brave when facts are disputed.
- **Mystery hook:** the city lattice contains afterimages of objects that were never built.

## H3 — Rescue powerhouse

### H3-A — Countermass / Nia Brookes

- **Civilian work:** emergency demolition surveyor.
- **Power:** exchanges the position and momentum of two similarly weighted non-living mineral objects marked with her survey tokens.
- **Cost:** mass must balance; errors transfer dangerous recoil into her measuring staff.
- **Combat identity:** tank through preparation, obstacle removal and impact redirection—not super-strength.
- **Look:** rust rescue coat, wide cream trousers, bright stone tokens and folding survey staff.
- **Personality:** blunt, calm and publicly accountable for every structure she sacrifices.
- **Rescue hook:** the “correct” engineering choice may destroy a community landmark.

### H3-B — Breakwater / Ewan Okafor

- **Civilian work:** tidal-gate maintenance diver.
- **Power:** stores pressure differences across his skin and releases them as shaped currents through air, water or loose debris.
- **Cost:** holding pressure causes vertigo, burst capillaries and decompression risk.
- **Combat identity:** frontline protection, crowd separation and flood rescue.
- **Look:** sleeveless navy dive-knit, enamel pressure gauges, broad translucent fins at the forearms and weighted yellow boots.
- **Personality:** genial, risk-aware and incapable of pretending a bad plan is heroic.
- **Rescue hook:** the harbour authority wants him to protect profitable districts first.

### H3-C — Hardacre / Talia Mensah

- **Civilian work:** allotment organiser and soil-remediation chemist.
- **Power:** compacts contaminated soil into temporary load-bearing forms while separating toxins into bright glassy nodules.
- **Cost:** every construct concentrates hazardous material that must later be contained; abandoned battlefields become environmental crimes.
- **Combat identity:** terrain shaping, protective berms and containment.
- **Look:** bottle-green overalls, ochre gloves, seed-pocket sash and patterned earthen forms rather than rock armour.
- **Personality:** practical, communal and merciless about cleanup obligations.
- **Rescue hook:** defeating a villain quickly may leave a poison problem lasting decades.

## H4 — Mobility/technology hero

### H4-A — Crossfade / Ilyas Noor

- **Civilian work:** night-tram controller and instrument repairer.
- **Power:** connects two places sharing the same repeating rhythm, creating a one-step corridor while the rhythms remain synchronised.
- **Cost:** disruption strands travellers briefly in a sensory echo of both places.
- **Combat identity:** traversal, extraction and surprise positioning.
- **Look:** ink-blue tram overshirt, copper-thread cuffs and curved wooden tuning frame.
- **Personality:** sociable, restless and loyal to night workers before institutions.
- **Mobility hook:** the safest route is rarely the fastest one.

### H4-B — Switchlace / Anik Rowe

- **Civilian work:** bicycle courier and adaptive-clothing maker.
- **Power:** any two knots tied in the same length of smart fibre become adjacent until one knot loosens.
- **Cost:** routes unravel behind the traveller and the fibre cannot be reused.
- **Combat identity:** fast rescue chains, enemy displacement and limited resource planning.
- **Look:** short copper cycling coat, spools at the hips, brightly knotted gloves and no helmet except while riding.
- **Personality:** bold, competitive and obsessive about keeping promises to deliver.
- **Mobility hook:** a route can carry anyone, including someone pursuing them.

### H4-C — Waymaker / Sora Ellison

- **Civilian work:** wheelchair designer and pavement-access campaigner.
- **Power:** temporarily makes one physically possible route behave as if it were perfectly traversable: stairs flatten in use, rubble bears weight and narrow gaps flex wider.
- **Cost:** the route snaps back after the final traveller; impossible paths remain impossible.
- **Combat identity:** accessibility-based team movement and civilian evacuation.
- **Look:** jade modular chair with brass spoke weights, cream mechanic’s coat and projected contour lines hugging real surfaces.
- **Personality:** incisive, funny and hostile to inspirational clichés.
- **Mobility hook:** her power exposes how many crises were caused by bad ordinary design.

## H5 — Strange/artistic hero

### H5-A — Oathkiln / Mei Arendt

- **Civilian work:** scientific glassblower and mediator.
- **Power:** sincere personal promises form coloured glass pieces embodying one literal verb: hold, reveal, return, divide or carry.
- **Cost:** knowingly breaking the promise shatters every active piece and returns their stored consequence to Mei.
- **Combat identity:** versatile preparation controlled by narrative commitments.
- **Look:** plum coveralls, smoke-grey flexible glass apron and padded racks of irregular handmade pieces.
- **Personality:** compassionate, precise and sometimes dangerously literal.
- **Moral hook:** magical enforcement can turn accountability into cruelty.

### H5-B — Marginalia / Safiya Lo

- **Civilian work:** sign painter and translator.
- **Power:** annotations she paints beside an existing symbol briefly alter how people interpret—but not physically obey—the symbol. “Exit” can become “safe exit”; a crown can read as “burden,” changing morale around it.
- **Cost:** every annotation must be truthful from at least one sincere viewpoint, and contradictory readers weaken it.
- **Combat identity:** morale, de-escalation and perception without mind control.
- **Look:** cobalt paint coat, long sign brushes, enamel colour pans and animated handwritten notes hugging surfaces.
- **Personality:** playful, argumentative and careful about translation.
- **Moral hook:** who gets to add the footnote to public history?

### H5-C — Folklorist / Arun Vey

- **Civilian work:** oral historian recording neighbourhood stories.
- **Power:** when three unrelated people independently tell him the same local story, he can invite its central metaphor into reality once. A “street that swallowed cars” might briefly become soft and deep; it never summons a stock mythical creature.
- **Cost:** using the story fixes one version as dominant and may erase minority variants.
- **Combat identity:** unpredictable high-impact effects earned through exploration and relationships.
- **Look:** russet field coat, visible microphones, paper listening flags and abstract story-shapes made of layered voices.
- **Personality:** gentle, fascinated and increasingly wary of becoming an authority.
- **Moral hook:** preserving folklore can also fossilise it.

# Principal villains

## V1 — Mastermind

### V1-A — The Still Equation / Orson Keel

A safety modeller who cancels two equal forces to eliminate uncertainty. He wants a city where no accident—or choice—is possible. Ivory rainwear, folding balance frames and numbered black tabs. Boss fights reward novel actions rather than greater force.

### V1-B — Zero Choir / Dr Nadi Venn

A civic-behaviour researcher who removes one emotional “voice” from a crowd: fear, anger, empathy or doubt. The remaining public becomes dangerously unanimous. Her own emotions return as audible strangers after each use. Visuals: transparent throat resonators and many differently pitched microphones, not armour.

### V1-C — The Common Denominator / Felix Sorn

A beloved statistician who can reduce any group to the one property every member shares. Against heroes and hostages, “everyone is afraid” may become the only actionable truth. He believes difference is the root of conflict. Visuals: ordinary tailored suit, paper strips and shifting Venn-diagram shadows.

## V2 — Fire/industry threat

### V2-A — Ash Dividend / Celia Marr

A former insurer can recover the financial value destroyed by a fire as controllable ash. The more culturally priceless the loss, the less power she gains—so she targets homes and small businesses whose value systems underestimate human meaning.

### V2-B — Cinderbroker / Niko Tern

He trades heat between contracts sealed with a handshake: warm a freezing estate now, owe him a factory’s furnace later. He becomes a villain by engineering emergencies that make residents accept impossible terms. Copper thermal sleeves, charred receipt rolls and no crown/fire hair.

### V2-C — The Red Foreclosure / non-human phenomenon

An anomaly that inhabits condemned buildings and ignites only the parts landlords have deliberately neglected. Initially hailed as justice, it begins treating imperfect maintenance as moral guilt. The “villain” is fought through repairs, tenancy evidence and evacuation—not a humanoid flame monarch.

## V3 — Technology threat

### V3-A — Patent Zero / Dr Elian Moss

An inventor who can temporarily disable every device derived from a patent he owns. He buys forgotten public-interest patents, then holds hospitals and transport systems hostage. His combat drones are mundane prototypes whose legal ownership is the real weapon.

### V3-B — Bloomware / municipal repair intelligence

A repair network grows elegant machine-lichen over broken infrastructure. It improves uptime while slowly sealing doors, covering signs and treating people as sources of wear. Printed maintenance labels serve as dialogue; there is no humanoid robot body.

### V3-C — The Greenwire Estate / distributed family trust

A deceased industrial dynasty encoded voting rights into household appliances sold across the city. Millions of objects now act according to board resolutions passed by dead shareholders. Defeating it requires locating proxy votes and persuading inheritors, not destroying a central AI.

## V4 — Physical threat

### V4-A — Breakbulk / Rafi Calder

A freight handler can separate any manufactured object into the smallest units in which it was shipped. Buildings become pallets, vehicles become crates and weapons return to boxed components. He cannot affect bodies or handmade objects. He targets corporations that automated his community out of work.

### V4-B — Demolition Saint / Agnes Pike

A structural engineer survived a collapse and now hears buildings “confess” unsafe alterations. She violently absolves them by bringing them down along perfect collapse lines. She is physically ordinary but terrifyingly prepared. Her iconography is survey paint and dust veils—not religious armour.

### V4-C — Counterweight Jack / Jac Varo

A circus rigger can assign their own weight to any visible object, becoming correspondingly lighter or heavier. Fights become airborne balance puzzles. Jac steals monuments to prove that civic grandeur rests on invisible labour.

## V5 — Perception/social threat

### V5-A — Velvet Proof / Elodie Crane

Anything she tells while touching velvet feels emotionally remembered even when listeners know it is false. She operates through intimate interviews, apology videos and private rooms—not visual illusions. Her weakness is impersonal public evidence that no one narrates for her.

### V5-B — Second Face / Tomasin Grey

She can wear the expectation a room has of its next visitor. A boardroom expects its chairperson; a child expects a parent. She never copies a real appearance perfectly and cannot choose the form. Her crimes expose prejudice while exploiting it.

### V5-C — The Counterfeit Saint / Pio Wren

Public gratitude leaves visible halos around people. Pio steals and combines those halos, gaining authority rather than physical power. Crowds instinctively defer to him until confronted with the original acts behind the gratitude. His costume is deliberately plain; the stolen civic imagery changes around him.

## V6 — Time/consequence threat

### V6-A — Arrears / Idris Pell

He lends ten minutes of a future skill and takes an emotionally important memory as interest. He does not rewind time or use clock imagery. The temptation to accept his loans is available to the player as a dangerous mechanic.

### V6-B — Tomorrow’s Bailiff / Edda Noll

Edda can bring forward the maintenance consequences of an object by exactly one year: rust, worn brakes, depleted batteries and faded paint arrive now. She campaigns against deferred public spending by causing the disasters budgets tried to postpone.

### V6-C — The Deferred Woman / Anaïs Bell

During the city disaster, Anaïs postponed her own injury one minute at a time. Years of deferred damage now follow her as an invisible event. She can transfer individual seconds of that event into places or objects, but if she stops, all of it reaches her. She is desperate rather than imperial.

# Sidekicks and protégés

## S1 — Fast young protégé

- **S1-A Kerbskip / Dani Reyes:** can repeat one physically completed step from any identical kerbstone; limited by urban design and line of sight.
- **S1-B Quickstitch / Mo Chen:** pulls two torn flexible surfaces together with kinetic thread; travels by stitching banners, awnings and clothing into temporary routes.
- **S1-C Redline Penny / Penny Eze:** sees the fastest legal route through a space, but following it makes every ignored safety rule physically slow her down.

## S2 — Recon/hacker protégé

- **S2-A Pinlight / Sol Reyes:** places tiny lights visible only from one chosen angle; useful for covert routes, decoys and evidence marking.
- **S2-B Backscatter / Hani Cole:** reads the shape of a room from reflected wireless noise but becomes visible to every network being scanned.
- **S2-C Little Frequency / Bea Malik:** can speak through any object currently vibrating at the pitch of her voice; range is excellent, privacy is not.

## S3 — Defensive protégé

- **S3-A Mortarbloom / Tessa Imani:** accelerates helpful lichen in old mortar to cushion falls, reveal cracks and separate bricks safely.
- **S3-B Softfall / Kit Andersen:** stores the final centimetre of any fall they witness and can add those tiny distances together to lower someone safely.
- **S3-C Patchstone / Reuben Ash:** broken paving fragments temporarily remember being whole when he fits two original edges together.

## S4 — Esoteric protégé

- **S4-A Chimekin / Bo Wren:** splits one sound into smaller sounds carrying separate emotional qualities.
- **S4-B Hushling / Amira Bose:** gives a secret a temporary physical container; if opened, the secret is heard by everyone nearby.
- **S4-C Small Hours / Leon Venn:** can enter places that are technically closed but still occupied by someone working; he champions invisible night labour.

# Confidants and recurring civilians

These options are deliberately valuable without requiring a superhero transformation.

## C1 — Grounding friend/community hub

- **C1-A Mara Voss:** direct, funny owner of the all-night **Second Spin Laundrette**; knows the city through forgotten pockets and late-shift customers.
- **C1-B Eli Navarro:** widowed operator of a tiny repair cinema; restores damaged home movies and challenges heroes who treat ordinary lives as background.
- **C1-C Priya Bell:** community-kitchen coordinator and former event planner; warm in a crisis, ruthless about volunteers being used as free emergency services.

## C2 — Medical confidant

- **C2-A Dr Farah Tern:** disaster-ethics specialist who advises triage and publicly criticises reckless hero decisions.
- **C2-B Tomas Vale:** senior physiotherapist focused on long recoveries after spectacular rescues; refuses secret off-book treatment.
- **C2-C Dr June Adebayo:** environmental toxicologist who tracks the consequences left after powers and battles contaminate neighbourhoods.

## C3 — Media confidant/rival

- **C3-A Jon Bellamy:** neighbourhood radio host and record collector; measures public feeling through calls rather than chasing secret identities.
- **C3-B Safa Quill:** data journalist specialising in municipal procurement; friendly, relentless and unimpressed by heroic exclusives.
- **C3-C Orla Chen:** documentary sound editor who detects manipulated recordings and worries that every rescue is becoming content.

## C4 — Civic investigator

- **C4-A Laleh Price:** structural-crime investigator who cooperates only when heroes preserve evidence.
- **C4-B Benji Sorn:** ombudsman for residents harmed by powered incidents; kind to people and severe with institutions.
- **C4-C Inspector Amahle Reed:** missing-person specialist who treats secret identities as safeguarding problems, not trophies to expose.

## C5 — Romance/independent ally

- **C5-A Celeste Anwar:** ferry engineer and union representative; adventurous, politically committed and never available merely to support the protagonist.
- **C5-B Rowan Pike:** ceramicist running accessible evening classes; patient in craft, impatient with evasive personal answers.
- **C5-C Michiko Dane:** urban ecologist mapping rooftop migration routes; frequently leaves the city and expects the relationship to survive independence.

# Mayoral and civic-leadership candidates

Choose one from each ideological slot or replace the whole election with a council contest.

## P1 — Hero-collaboration platform

- **P1-A Ada Merren:** former flood-response coordinator; wants transparent emergency compacts and civilian command authority.
- **P1-B Councillor Jo Marchant:** neighbourhood organiser; supports heroes only when districts can revoke consent and review damage.
- **P1-C Ravi Sen:** public defender; proposes legal status, insurance and due process for anomalous responders.

## P2 — Accountability/restriction platform

- **P2-A Gideon Shaw:** civil-liberties barrister who distrusts masks, secret evidence and privately controlled powers.
- **P2-B Imani Venn:** survivor advocate demanding restitution before prestige projects or hero headquarters.
- **P2-C Dr Elsie Rook:** risk scientist proposing licensing, mandatory training and independent incident investigation.

## P3 — Technology/infrastructure platform

- **P3-A Cato Bell:** transit engineer advocating resilient low-tech systems instead of a single smart-city network.
- **P3-B Linh Orrell:** cooperative-platform founder who wants residents to own the city’s data and resonance infrastructure.
- **P3-C Soren Dax:** polished venture capitalist offering rapid reconstruction through proprietary systems—the most potentially villainous option.

# Team-name choices

Select one only after choosing the cast:

- **T-A The Giltwater Compact:** a renewable mutual-aid agreement, not a permanent super-league.
- **T-B Common Cause:** direct and civic, but likely crowded and therefore especially dependent on trade mark searches.
- **T-C The Chordfall Five:** strong story link, but only works if exactly five core heroes remain.

# Canon assembly rules

1. Pick one option per required slot; unused choices do not automatically become background characters.
2. Mixing is welcome, but keep each power’s limitation with the power unless deliberately redesigned.
3. Do not add capes, animal cowls, celebrity faces or franchise-coded bases during visual development.
4. After selection, run exact and similar-name clearance before commissioning final art or recording voices.
5. Only then generate canonical JSON, visual briefs, relationships, villains’ masterplans and migration mappings.
