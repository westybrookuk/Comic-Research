# Proposed Five-Character Cast — Worldwide Preliminary Clearance Review

**Review date:** 5 September 2026  
**Territory requested:** worldwide  
**Material reviewed:** the proposed names, aliases, powers, costumes, signature objects, phrases and institutions supplied for the five-person starter cast

> **Decision summary:** Do **not** commercially release this cast unchanged. Replace **The Vector**, **The Hush** and **Ticker** before commissioning final art, voice work, packaging or marketing. **Chloe Rivera** and **Clara Vey** can provisionally continue. Rename **The Meridian Café** if it will be a prominent or merchandised brand. This is a practical knockout search, not a legal opinion or a guarantee of clearance.

## 2. Why the three aliases fail the practical release gate

### A. The Vector / Adrian Lee

#### Collision

Marvel’s official U-Foes page identifies **Vector** as a member of that group. The character record describes Simon Utrecht/Vector as able to block and repel matter and energy, directing it away from himself. The proposed hero redirects the trajectory of objects already in motion. These are not identical rule sets, but consumers encounter:

1. the exact super-name **Vector**;
2. in the same superhero field;
3. attached in each case to the directional manipulation of moving matter/forces; and
4. in media in which Marvel’s Vector has already appeared, including television and a video game.

That combination is materially riskier than use of the dictionary word in an unrelated field. The proposed blue/ivory/brass palette may differ from Marvel imagery, but a different palette does not cure the name-and-power collision.

The name is also commercially crowded outside comics. Nekki’s *Vector* has been sold on Steam since 2013, and *Vector Light*, *Vectors* and *Vector Wave* also occupy game-store search results. This adds discoverability, title, app-store and advertising problems even if the character alias is not the game title.

#### Recommendation

- **Replace “The Vector”.** Do not rely on the definite article.
- **Adrian Lee may provisionally remain.** Searches did not identify the exact proposed hero pairing. “Adrian” alone is not proprietary, although superhero audiences may know DC’s Adrian Chase; this is too weak by itself to require change.
- Keep the broad trajectory-redirection idea only if its rules, origin, limitations, effects, poses, dialogue and story expression are independently developed.
- Describe the ability internally as **trajectory deflection** or **momentum routing**, not “Vector powers” or “vector manipulation”.
- Do not use Marvel’s green/yellow presentation, U-Foes framing, radiation origin, outward repulsion pose language or copied effect art.
- Clear any final arrow/compass emblem by image search. Directional lines, arrows and compass points are crowded design territory.

**Residual risk after a strong rename and original expression:** low–medium.

### B. The Hush / Dr. Lucien Orr

#### Collision

DC’s official character page identifies **Hush**, Thomas Elliot, as a major Batman villain. DC also continues to exploit *Batman: Hush* in film, books, toys, trading cards, video games and a 2025 standalone board game, *DC Super Heroes United: Batman Hush*. The proposed character is likewise a masked male principal villain in superhero entertainment. The phrase **The Hush** remains aurally and conceptually HUSH; the added article is not a meaningful commercial distinction.

The proposed black coat and blank porcelain mask differ from Hush’s bandaged face, and information becoming unavailable to perception differs from Thomas Elliot’s skill set. Those differences reduce copyright-character similarity but do not make the alias sensible to launch.

The power presentation has a separate reference point: the BBC describes Doctor Who’s **Silence** as beings who edit themselves from an observer’s memory once out of view. Marvel’s **Blindspot** has also removed memories of her existence. Memory erasure or perception suppression is a general fictional idea and is not monopolised, but presenting Lucien as a faceless being whom observers cannot perceive/remember would move the total expression closer to occupied territory.

#### Recommendation

- **Replace “The Hush” completely.** Do not use Hush, The Hush, Hushed, Hushman or another cosmetic variant.
- Consider changing **Orr** as well. DC has a separate character called Mr. Orr and a separate Lucien; neither alone is a strong conflict, but there is no benefit in preserving a cluster of DC-adjacent identifiers after adopting DC’s exact villain alias.
- Keep Lucien a communications/information theorist rather than a surgeon; do not echo Thomas Elliot’s medical history, Bruce Wayne relationship, facial surgery, bandages or revenge plot.
- Prefer a power that suppresses **encoded/transmitted information**—records, captions, signals, labels or testimony—rather than one that erases the villain himself from memory when unseen.
- If human memory is affected, establish original constraints, costs and visual grammar. Avoid the precise “visible while observed, entirely forgotten when out of sight” mechanism.
- Replace the blank face with a distinctive non-bandage interface or exposed face. If a mask is essential, avoid wrapped/bandaged contours and Batman-like noir staging.

**Residual risk after alias, surname/presentation and power-boundary changes:** low–medium.

### C. Ticker / Niko Serrat

#### Collision

The risk is cumulative:

- Marvel has a minor Earth-616 character whose exact alias is **Ticker**.
- Steam has sold a game titled **Ticker** since September 2024; the developer and publisher are also displayed as Ticker.
- DC’s modern Clock King, Tem, can see approximately 4.6692 seconds into the future and exploit that advantage in combat.
- Marvel’s clock-named **Ticktock** is a precognitive character.
- The proposal combines an exact occupied alias, a teenage clockmaker, analogue timepiece imagery, a pocket watch and a very short-horizon combat advantage.

“Experiences one second twice” is a sharper and more interesting mechanic than generic future sight: it can be expressed as a second experiential pass rather than seeing a future timeline. Copyright does not protect the broad idea, but the game’s written rules, scenes, animations and art must not copy another work’s expression. The alias and packaging remain the immediate avoidable problems.

#### Recommendation

- **Replace “Ticker”.** This is required even before considering the Marvel character because there is an exact current game-store title.
- **Niko Serrat may provisionally remain;** no meaningful exact collision surfaced.
- Keep the mechanic, but define it as **re-experiencing the immediately completed second with one opportunity to change Niko’s own action**. Do not describe it as seeing several seconds into the future.
- Give it a hard cost and a non-clock visual effect—for example, sensory doubling, displaced sound or duplicated after-images—rather than floating clock faces.
- A clockmaker background and one inherited watch can remain, but reduce the stack: do not combine clock-name + clock costume + gears + ticking dialogue + stopwatch UI + precognition.
- Treat `SECONDHAND` as prop text, not the sidekick, game mode, app, toy or merchandise brand unless separately cleared. A more distinctive invented prop name would be easier to protect.

**Residual risk after alias and visual-language changes:** medium-low; lower if the clock imagery is also reduced.

## 3. The two civilian/political characters

### Chloe Rivera

The exact search principally surfaced real people, not a recognisable fictional entertainment property. Ordinary personal names receive no blanket exclusivity. The practical concerns are privacy, publicity/personality rights and false endorsement if the finished character copies a real person’s image, career, biography, voice or social-media identity.

**Keep, subject to:**

- an exact-name search in the project’s main launch countries immediately before release;
- an independent face and biography, not an AI prompt based on a living person;
- no marketing implying that a real Chloe Rivera endorsed the game; and
- replacing **The Meridian Café** if it becomes a map label, venue logo, merchandise line or major recurring brand.

The café wording is crowded. Search results include Café Meridian in Durham, Meridian Cafe in Puyallup, The Meridian Cafe in Louisville and Meridian Café & Bar in Manchester. Geographic separation may matter in a legal analysis, but a fictional global game gains little from adopting a weak name already used by hospitality businesses.

### Clara Vey

No material exact `"Clara Vey"` entertainment-character or political-persona collision surfaced in this review. “Clara” alone appears across many works but is not enough to create a meaningful conflict. The commissioner-to-mayoral-candidate arc is an ordinary role concept.

**Keep, subject to:**

- final word-mark searches for `CLARA VEY`, `VEY` and phonetic variants in relevant territories;
- avoiding the likeness, biography or recognisable campaign material of a real candidate;
- exact and similar phrase searches for the final slogan; and
- visual searches of the completed campaign device.

The exact slogan **“A CITY THAT CAN STAND ON ITS OWN”** did not produce a meaningful hit in the web knockout search. It should nevertheless be treated as campaign copy rather than assumed exclusive property.

## 4. Copyright, trade mark and unfair-competition analysis

### Copyright / character expression

UK government guidance states that copyright protects **expression, not the underlying idea**. Broad concepts such as redirecting trajectories, suppressing information, repeating a second, operating a café or running for mayor therefore are not themselves reserved. Finished prose, dialogue, artwork, animation, music, code and sufficiently developed character expression can be protected.

The practical test is cumulative. A new name does not permit copying another character’s costume, origin, poses, story relationships, signature scenes or dialogue. Conversely, sharing a broad power concept does not by itself establish infringement when independently expressed.

### Trade marks / passing off / unfair competition

Character names can function as source identifiers when used prominently on games, comics, expansions, toys or merchandise. Official USPTO guidance says marks need not be identical: similarity in sound, appearance, meaning or commercial impression can matter, together with the relationship between goods/services. UK passing off and analogous unfair-competition/false-designation rules may also protect goodwill even where a registration search is quiet.

That is why the strongest problems here are **Hush in superhero entertainment**, **Vector plus directional-force powers**, and **Ticker plus exact game-store use and clock-foresight framing**. This report does **not** claim that any named rights-holder would necessarily win litigation. It concludes that adopting these avoidable conflicts is commercially imprudent.

### Finished visuals and quoted lines

No final production art was supplied for image comparison. Colour lists and generic concepts are not enough to clear a costume or logo. The final character sheets should be searched visually and reviewed side-by-side—not prompted from protected character art.

Only two proposed textual devices were available for exact searching in this review: `SECONDHAND` and `A CITY THAT CAN STAND ON ITS OWN`. Any additional quips, catchphrases, mission titles, faction names or institutions must be added to the clearance schedule before recording or release.

## 6. Required next clearance stage

Because the requested market is “worldwide”, nobody should label these names globally safe from this report. WIPO itself notes that true multinational searches are complex; its Global Brand Database does not cover every mark, and search output is not a legal opinion.

Before public announcement, have a qualified trade mark/IP lawyer or professional search provider run and interpret:

1. **UKIPO** — exact, similar, contains-word and contains-string searches;
2. **EUIPO/TMview** — EU and participating national records, including image search;
3. **WIPO Global Brand Database** — Madrid/international and participating collections;
4. **USPTO** — federal word/design records plus US common-law use;
5. national registers for the project’s priority revenue, development, publishing and merchandising territories;
6. common-law sources, company registers, app/game stores, comics catalogues, streaming databases, social platforms and web/domain use; and
7. image searches for all completed emblems, costume silhouettes, masks and prop designs.

At minimum, search relevant specifications in and across:

- **Class 9:** downloadable game software, recorded media and electronic publications;
- **Class 16:** comics, printed publications, art and stationery;
- **Class 28:** games, toys and action figures;
- **Class 41:** entertainment, online games and publishing;
- **Class 25:** clothing/costume merchandise; and
- hospitality classes only if the fictional café will become a real promotional venue, food/drink line or licensed café brand.

The UKIPO cross-search list specifically links game-related searching across Classes 9, 28 and 41. Search names both alone and in combinations, including plurals, articles, phonetic equivalents and translations/transliterations important to launch markets.

## Bottom line

The proposed cast has three avoidable, commercially serious collisions at its superhero core. **Do not use The Vector, The Hush or Ticker unchanged.** The underlying creative seeds can be salvaged with new names and sharper differentiation. **Chloe Rivera and Clara Vey are reasonable candidates to carry forward to formal clearance**, while **The Meridian Café** and `SECONDHAND` should remain minor fictional details or be replaced with more distinctive terms.

Only qualified counsel reviewing the final names, artwork, scripts, target markets and professional search results can provide a legal clearance opinion.
