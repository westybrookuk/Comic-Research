# Preliminary IP Risk Audit — *Vanguard: The Greatest Hero*

**Audit date:** 3 September 2026  
**Release scope assumed:** Worldwide game release, with particular attention to the UK, US and EU  
**Materials reviewed:** all files in this research pack (JSON data, copied source snapshots, prompt bibles, location guide, music prompts and the two supplied artwork files)

> **Important:** This is a practical creative clearance screen, not legal advice, a legal opinion, or a guarantee that a claim will or will not be made. Copyright, trade marks, passing off/unfair competition, publicity/personality rights and platform contracts are different rights. Trade mark clearance is territorial and class-specific. Before launch, a qualified IP solicitor/trade mark attorney should search the final title, logo, principal character names and merchandise categories in every important market.

## Executive conclusion

## **Do not release the present version as-is.**

The project contains several direct names from established superhero properties, several explicit franchise/location labels, two character packages that closely reproduce Superman/Batman signifiers, a villain literally named from two linked Marvel magnetic characters, and prompts requiring recognisable celebrity faces. These are not merely vague similarities inherent in the superhero genre.

The most urgent changes are:

1. Remove **Dr. Magneto Polaris / Dr. Polaris** completely and replace the name, costume and magnetic presentation.
2. Replace **Batcave, Blackgate, Danger Room, Cerebro-Core, S.T.A.R. Labs and GCPD** everywhere.
3. Rename/rework **Madame Mirage, Circuit Breaker, Chronos/Doctor Chronos, Iron Juggernaut, Nocturne, Solaris, Prism, Lifeline, Nightblade, Aura and Doctor Megawatt**.
4. Rebuild **Vanguard Primus/Alex Cross** so he is not a Superman package (journalist, telephone-booth reveal, cape, chest crest, flight/strength, metropolis, Henry Cavill likeness).
5. Rebuild the bat vigilante material (**Nocturne** and the alternate **Shadow Tempest/Victor Drake**) so it is not a Batman package.
6. Delete all celebrity “face blend/identical” anchors and all production prompts naming franchises, studios, living artists, composers or a specific artist’s style.
7. Do not use a replica **Comics Code Authority** seal or market generated art as “authentic Marvel” material.
8. Reconcile the two competing casts before clearance: the core data and Vibes prompt suites contain different hero identities.

## Risk scale

- **Critical:** direct established identifier or a highly recognisable protected character/franchise package. Change before any public demo, store listing, trailer or merchandising.
- **High:** strong collision in name, role, power or surrounding presentation; change is strongly recommended and professional clearance is required if retained.
- **Medium:** crowded or suggestive term; possibly usable only after registry/common-law searches and differentiation.
- **Low:** no material mainstream collision identified in this preliminary review; still search before adopting as a brand.

A matching name alone is not necessarily copyright infringement. It may, however, create trade mark, passing-off or unfair-competition risk. Copyright risk rises when a distinctive combination of biography, appearance, powers, props, locations and scenes is reproduced.

# 4. Explicit third-party references in production prompts

The prompt documents are not safe production specifications in their current form. They explicitly request:

- “Marvel Iron Man style,” “authentic vintage Marvel Bronze Age,” “Pixar-meets-Marvel,” “Avengers Endgame hybrid”;
- Batman, Batman: TAS, Gotham City, GCPD, Batcave, Batman Tumbler and “Frank Miller Dark Knight style”;
- *Fast & Furious*, *Phoenix Wright*, *Blade Runner*, *Dark Souls*, *Arcane*, *Solo Leveling* and named animation studios;
- styles of identifiable artists including Alex Ross, Jim Lee, Jack Kirby, Neal Adams, Phil Noto, Greg Capullo, Tim Sale, Lee Bermejo, Artgerm/Stanley Lau, Cliff Chiang, Fiona Staples, Esad Ribić, Joe Madureira, Mike Mignola, David Finch and others;
- composer blends including Hans Zimmer and John Williams;
- celebrity likeness blends listed in the critical table.

A style reference is not automatically a copyright infringement by itself, and general style is not the same thing as copying protected expression. The commercial output may nevertheless reproduce protected character elements, a particular source image, a recognisable person, or platform-restricted material. The prompts also provide evidence that imitation was intended, which is unhelpful if an output is challenged.

**Production rule:** replace every third-party proper noun with neutral visual/music attributes. For example, specify line weight, palette, camera, lighting, instrumentation, tempo and mood—never a franchise, living artist, performer or celebrity. Retain generation logs, model/version details, licences and human revision records. Have a human artist conduct a similarity review of every final asset.

# 5. Other non-character issues

## Comics Code Authority presentation

The cover prompt requests a replica “APPROVED BY THE COMICS CODE AUTHORITY” box and calls the result an “authentic vintage Marvel” cover. Do not use either representation. Create an unmistakably fictional in-world approval device with different wording, shape and placement, or omit it.

## Real people

The actor-locked prompt suite creates obvious commercial likeness risk. Depending on the market and facts, claims may arise through passing off/false endorsement, publicity/personality rights, privacy/data protection and advertising rules. Blending two celebrities is not a reliable defence if each remains recognisable.

## AI service terms and asset provenance

The pack identifies Vibes.ai/Suno-oriented prompts but contains no completed AI character art or music licences. Before using generated output, preserve:

- the service and model/version;
- account/plan and terms in force on generation date;
- prompt, seed and source images;
- proof of commercial-use permission;
- contributor/contractor assignment agreements;
- evidence that uploads and voice samples were authorised;
- human edits and layered source files.

Do not assume that permission from an AI platform cures infringement or likeness risk.

## Supplied artwork

The pack states that no actual character/location artwork was supplied. The only raster file is described as a stock Vite template placeholder, and the SVG is a favicon. Therefore this audit cannot approve any final character design, logo, cover, animation, music or voice output. Every final production asset needs a second visual/audio clearance pass.

# 7. Safer redesign principles

1. **Create from a differentiating thesis, not from a known hero with details swapped.** Write each lead’s unique contradiction, social role, limitation and visual geometry first.
2. **Avoid one-to-one ecosystems.** A billionaire bat vigilante plus cave, butler-like support, armoured car, police signal, asylum and gothic city remains recognisable even if every noun is renamed.
3. **Use invented, searchable aliases.** Two ordinary dictionary words are often crowded. Test pronunciation, spelling and translations.
4. **Separate powers from famous visual grammar.** A solar hero need not wear a cape or chest sunburst; a technologist need not use red/gold nanotech armour and repulsor poses.
5. **Document independent creation.** Keep dated concept notes, rejected alternatives, sketches and search results.
6. **Clear names before final art and voice spend.** Finalise one canon roster; the current core data and Vibes suites conflict.

# 8. Professional clearance checklist before release

For the final game title, studio name, logo, five principal heroes, principal villains, teams and major recurring locations:

1. Search exact, phonetic, translated and visually similar marks through:
   - UK IPO;
   - EUIPO/TMview;
   - USPTO;
   - WIPO Global Brand Database;
   - national registers in other priority markets.
2. Search at least Classes **9** (downloadable games/software), **41** (entertainment), **28** (games/toys), **16** (comics/printed matter) and **25** (clothing), tailored by counsel.
3. Conduct unregistered/common-law searches: game stores, app stores, Steam/console stores, comics databases, company registers, domains, social handles, crowdfunding and merchandise.
4. Obtain a written legal clearance opinion on the final shortlist.
5. Commission a second visual comparison after final model sheets, logos, cover art, UI, music and trailers exist.
6. Obtain assignments/releases from every writer, artist, actor, composer and contractor.
