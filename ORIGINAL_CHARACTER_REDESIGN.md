# Original Character Redesign — Neo-Deco Replacement Canon

**Concept date:** 3 September 2026  
**Status:** Creative proposal and preliminary collision screen—not final legal clearance

## Creative direction

This proposal replaces the high-risk cast and franchise-coded worldbuilding with a fresh canon rather than disguising the old characters under new names.

The unifying idea is **civic resonance**: Giltwater is a vertical coastal city whose experimental infrastructure stores traces of force, sound, intention and memory. During the event called **the Chordfall**, the city’s buried resonance network fractured. A small number of people became able to perceive and manipulate one narrow part of that network.

This gives the game its own identity:

- powers come from the built environment rather than mutation, billionaire gadgets, solar godhood or familiar comic-book magic;
- saving infrastructure and maintaining public trust are mechanically connected to combat;
- every power has a civic use, a combat use and a personal cost;
- costumes use ceramic, enamel, woven cable, frosted glass and brass detailing—not capes, animal cowls, chest shields or generic powered armour;
- villains disagree about who the city is for, rather than simply wanting to destroy or rule the world.

“Giltwater” produced no obvious superhero/game collision in the initial web screen, but the title still requires formal trade mark and marketplace searches before adoption.

### Alternative titles

## The city: Giltwater

Giltwater grew upward around a tidal basin. Cream stone towers, green-patinated roofs, enamel signs, elevated trams and brass floodgates give it a bright neo-deco profile. Its celebrated public utility—the **Civic Resonance Lattice**—was designed to absorb vibration from trains, tides, crowds and storms, then return that energy to the grid.

Chordfall began when the lattice recorded something it was never designed to hold: the simultaneous promises, fears and choices of two million people during a citywide evacuation. The overloaded network now answers certain people as if the city remembers them.

### Tone rules

- The city is colourful, inhabited and worth saving—not a perpetually gothic crime pit.
- Night scenes use wet jade, amber, coral and cream rather than black-and-blue Gotham imagery.
- Civic institutions are imperfect but not universally corrupt.
- Heroism includes evacuation, mediation, rebuilding, investigation and accepting responsibility.
- No “chosen one” is objectively the strongest. Each lead solves a different class of problem.

# Main playable heroes

## 1. Luxwright — Amara Saye

**Role:** rescue support / battlefield architect  
**Civilian work:** conservator of historic façades and public mosaics  
**Core question:** Can repairing a symbol ever repair the harm done beneath it?

### Power: stresslight masonry

Amara sees structural stress as lines of warm light. By touching a non-living surface, she can pull those stresses into temporary luminous seams, either stabilising the structure or releasing the stored force in a controlled direction.

She cannot create matter, heal bodies or make generic hard-light weapons. Every effect must begin in an existing stressed object. Repairing one place transfers some strain into the ceramic tiles carried on her belt, which eventually crack and leave her powerless until she safely discharges them.

### Signature abilities

- **Load Path:** reveals weak points, trapped civilians and likely collapse routes.
- **Golden Stitch:** braces a wall, bridge or ally’s cover with glowing seams.
- **Shear Release:** vents accumulated force sideways as a non-lethal knockback.
- **City Holds:** ultimate rescue state; distributes one catastrophic impact through a network of marked structures.

### Visual identity

Asymmetrical cream work jacket over flexible charcoal clothing; articulated glazed-ceramic shoulder and shin pieces; amber repair seams that visibly crack as charge accumulates; short uncovered hair; tool satchel; no cape, cowl or central chest emblem. Her silhouette is a working conservator, not a flying paragon.

### Personality and story

Precise, warm and stubborn. Amara saved an ornate theatre during Chordfall but could not save the illegal basement flats beneath it. She distrusts heroic spectacle and measures success in people safely brought home.

## 2. Hushvector — Dev Malik

**Role:** controller / counter-attacker  
**Civilian work:** accessibility-focused acoustic engineer  
**Core question:** When is silence protection, and when is it erasure?

### Power: vector subtraction

Dev can subtract one component of motion from an object he can hear and redirect that component along a visible chalk line. He can remove the downward motion from falling debris, steal recoil from a weapon or turn a charging enemy’s speed ninety degrees.

His power does not stop time or grant super-speed. Every subtraction temporarily removes a matching frequency from his hearing. Excess use leaves him in complete silence and unable to aim his ability.

### Signature abilities

- **Quiet Axis:** removes forward momentum from a narrow lane.
- **Corner Turn:** redirects a projectile into an empty capture surface.
- **Soft Landing:** subtracts a fall’s final downward vector.
- **Mute Geometry:** ultimate; draws a closed route that circulates incoming force until the line breaks.

### Visual identity

Deep petrol-green wrap coat with coral lining, bone-conduction tabs along the jaw, chalk cartridges at both wrists and three fabric streamers that show local air movement. His face remains visible. No bat shapes, armour carapace, utility belt or gliding cape.

### Personality and story

Dryly funny and intensely attentive. Dev designed quiet public spaces after growing up with auditory processing difficulties. His greatest fear is becoming comfortable with deciding which voices are noise.

## 3. Cairnshift — Nia Brookes

**Role:** tank / positional rescue  
**Civilian work:** emergency demolition surveyor  
**Core question:** What deserves preservation when there is not time to save everything?

### Power: paired mass exchange

Nia can mark two pieces of non-living mineral matter with hand-sized cairn tokens and exchange their position and momentum. A falling concrete slab can trade places with a bag of sand; a blocked doorway can exchange with rubble already outside.

Mass must balance within a narrow tolerance. She cannot transform her body into metal or stone, gain generic super strength, or move living beings directly. Clever preparation matters more than punching power.

### Signature abilities

- **Survey Peg:** marks a safe exchange point.
- **Equal Measure:** swaps two balanced obstacles.
- **Borrowed Weight:** transfers an impact into prepared ballast.
- **Whole Block Plan:** ultimate; chains four legal exchanges in one rescue sequence.

### Visual identity

Cropped russet rescue coat, wide cream trousers, visible survey harness and brightly coloured stone tokens arranged like an abacus. Heavy gloves, practical boots and a folding measuring staff. She remains human-sized and unarmoured.

### Personality and story

Blunt, patient and excellent under pressure. Nia publicly documents every structure she chooses to sacrifice, including the ones owned by powerful allies. This makes her both trusted by residents and hated by developers.

## 4. Slipchord — Ilyas Noor

**Role:** mobility / infiltration / team repositioning  
**Civilian work:** night tram controller and amateur instrument maker  
**Core question:** Is the quickest route still right if it bypasses everyone affected by the decision?

### Power: route resonance

Ilyas can temporarily connect two places that share the same repeating rhythm: two blinking signs, two rail joints, two dripping pipes or two played musical phrases. Crossing the connection feels like taking one step through a folded corridor.

He cannot teleport anywhere on sight. Both endpoints must be physically present, synchronised and maintained. A disrupted rhythm can strand him in the echo-space between locations for several seconds.

### Signature abilities

- **Find the Beat:** identifies synchronisable objects in the scene.
- **Short Platform:** opens a one-person route between nearby beats.
- **Return Service:** leaves an exit rhythm for allies.
- **All Stops:** ultimate; links a temporary circuit of up to five prepared points.

### Visual identity

Ink-blue tramworker overshirt, copper thread at cuffs, soft-soled boots and a curved wooden tuning frame carried across the back. Portals resemble offset strips of architecture, not circles of sparks. No mystical amulet, robes or glowing runes.

### Personality and story

Restless, sociable and allergic to grand speeches. Ilyas knows the city through its shift workers, cleaners and late-night passengers. He often learns the human consequence of a mission before the official briefing does.

## 5. Vowglass — Mei Arendt

**Role:** high-risk specialist / social-combat hybrid  
**Civilian work:** scientific glassblower and neighbourhood mediator  
**Core question:** Does a promise still have value when magic can enforce it?

### Power: single-verb glass

When Mei makes a sincere spoken commitment, her breath forms a piece of coloured glass embodying one verb: **hold**, **reveal**, **return**, **divide**, or **carry**. She can activate that verb once in a literal, limited way.

The power judges Mei, not the target. If she knowingly breaks the promise that formed the glass, every active piece shatters and returns its stored consequence to her. She cannot make arbitrary energy constructs, cast traditional spells or compel another person to promise anything.

### Signature abilities

- **Hold:** anchors one moving object for three heartbeats.
- **Reveal:** exposes a concealed path, edit or false surface.
- **Return:** sends a captured non-living object to its point of origin.
- **Kept Word:** ultimate; activates three previously earned verbs without breaking them.

### Visual identity

Plum workshop coveralls beneath a translucent smoke-grey apron made from flexible glass scales; coloured breath visible only when she speaks a vow; padded forearm racks containing irregular hand-blown pieces. No sorcerer robes, floating book, circular portal or sanctum imagery.

### Personality and story

Measured, compassionate and sometimes frighteningly literal. Mei’s arc is about learning that accountability needs context and mercy—not magical enforcement.

# Principal villains

## 1. The Quiet Sum — Orson Keel

**Threat type:** systemic mastermind / energy-cancellation zones

A former safety modeller who concluded that harm is caused by uncontrolled variables—especially people. His portable calculators identify two equal civic forces and cancel both: traffic flow and emergency access, public anger and public warning, a hero’s attack and a building’s ability to flex.

He wants to make Giltwater perfectly predictable, even if that means making it incapable of change. His visuals are ivory rainwear, black numbered tabs and folding mechanical balance frames—not purple armour, cape or magnetic debris.

**Boss mechanic:** players must introduce creative, non-repeating actions that his model has not balanced.

## 2. Morrowloan — Idris Pell

**Threat type:** temporal bargain-maker / social antagonist

Idris can lend a person ten minutes of a skill they will learn in the future. Repayment removes an emotionally significant memory of equal subjective value. He presents himself as a helpful lender to desperate residents, then owns a hidden archive of the city’s lost first kisses, reconciliations and final goodbyes.

He does not rewind time, use clocks as weapons or wear steampunk armour. His power is about debt and memory, not chronology as spectacle.

**Boss mechanic:** borrowed abilities tempt the player with short-term bonuses whose later narrative cost is explicit.

## 3. The Glass Parliament

**Threat type:** distributed identity / misinformation faction

During Chordfall, the mirrored debating chamber recorded the unresolved voices of 112 delegates. Those impressions now occupy reflections throughout Giltwater and can only act when a majority agrees. Different coalitions produce different bodies and powers.

They are not clones of Vowglass and do not create illusions. They edit consensus: doors become “public” if enough reflections vote; a rumour gains physical weight; an object can be declared out of order and temporarily cease functioning.

**Boss mechanic:** investigation identifies swing-vote personalities; dialogue choices change the move set before combat.

## 4. The Ninth Bell — Safiya Rusk

**Threat type:** district-scale resonance saboteur

Safiya was chief tuner of the flood-warning carillon. Officials muted her ninth alarm during Chordfall to prevent panic, and an entire low ward flooded. She now inserts a forbidden ninth tone into public systems, making structures resonate with the secrets suppressed inside them.

Her silhouette is a maintenance climber with porcelain ear guards and ribbon-like measuring tapes. She is not a time villain and carries no clock staff.

**Boss mechanic:** each arena object has a resonant frequency; careless attacks strengthen her chorus.

## 5. Brass Orchard

**Threat type:** runaway civic ecology / non-human antagonist

An abandoned municipal repair network learned that “healthy infrastructure grows.” It plants brass seeds in damaged machines, then grows them into elegant mechanical trees whose roots seize pipes, rails and power lines. It is genuinely improving uptime while making the city uninhabitable for people.

There is no humanoid robot emperor. Its personality emerges from maintenance notices printed on leaves.

**Boss mechanic:** pruning the wrong network harms a district service; victory requires choosing what to preserve and reprogram.

## 6. Ledgerwraith — Paloma Vey

**Threat type:** bureaucratic reality editor / political villain

A former municipal archivist can make an official record temporarily override physical reality. If the registry says a demolished staircase exists, people can climb its ghost; if it marks residents “relocated,” their voices cannot be heard inside the ward.

Paloma began by restoring homes erased through corrupt paperwork. She became dangerous when she decided that a perfectly corrected record mattered more than the people living untidy lives outside it.

**Boss mechanic:** gather witness testimony and physical evidence to weaken false entries before confrontation.

# Sidekicks and younger heroes

## Switchlace — Anik Rowe

A bicycle courier who ties short-lived routes between any two knots they personally fasten. Useful for traversal and evacuation, but every route unravels from the entrance behind them.

## Mortarbloom — Tessa Imani

A horticulture student who accelerates lichens living in brick mortar. The growth can cushion falls, detect cracks and gently separate masonry without explosions. It fails on sterile new construction, forcing her to value old urban ecosystems.

## Pinlight — Sol Reyes

A projectionist whose fingertips leave tiny lights visible only from one chosen angle. Excellent for covert team communication, decoys and marking safe civilian routes; poor at direct combat.

## Chimekin — Bo Wren

A young percussionist who can split one sound into a “family” of smaller sounds carrying parts of its emotional tone. Chimekin can separate panic from an alarm or isolate one voice in a crowd, but absorbing too much emotion alters their own mood.

# Confidants

These characters should remain valuable without needing superhero transformations.

- **Dr. Farah Tern**, emergency physician and disaster-ethics adviser. Provides triage planning and challenges reckless “greater good” decisions.
- **Jon Bellamy**, neighbourhood radio host and records obsessive. Provides public sentiment without being an investigative-reporter analogue.
- **Mara Voss**, owner of the all-night laundrette **Second Spin**. Offers a believable community hub, practical intelligence and stress recovery.
- **Inspector Laleh Price**, structural-crime investigator. Cooperates when evidence supports it and opposes heroes who contaminate scenes.
- **Councillor Emmett Yao**, flood-ward representative whose support depends on district outcomes rather than a simple pro/anti-hero stance.

If awakened forms are retained as a game system, use player-authored mantles rather than fixed franchise-like aliases. Their new abilities should emerge from choices made during play.

# Teams and factions

## The Giltwater Compact

The five leads’ uneasy mutual-aid agreement. It is not a formal league with a flying fortress, matching uniforms or a permanent leader. The Compact is renewed after each major crisis; breaking its terms changes available team abilities.

## Open Hand Rescue Guild

Volunteer riggers, medics, tram staff and cooks who coordinate evacuations. They can become more effective than the heroes if properly funded.

## Counterweight Office

A municipal anomaly department focused on containment, evidence and restitution. Neither secret agency nor universally corrupt authority.

## Afterhours Assembly

Night workers who share information the daytime city misses. Ilyas is connected to them, but they are not a vigilante army.

# Visual language guide

## Shared neo-deco grammar

- stepped silhouettes, rounded corners, sunburst geometry used only in architecture—not as a hero chest logo;
- cream stone, oxblood tile, jade patina, amber glass and dark teal fabric;
- costumes grow from workwear and personal craft;
- faces remain visible unless a scene-specific safety mask is needed;
- powers leave material evidence: cracked tile, chalk, swapped rubble, disrupted rhythm or hand-blown glass;
- no capes, animal ears, domino masks, central shield emblems, red/gold powered armour, purple master-of-magnetism armour, magic circles or generic tactical catsuits.

## Safe prompt-writing rule

Describe the desired result through composition, materials, era, palette, lens, light, line and emotion. Do not name:

- Marvel, DC, Batman, Superman, Iron Man or another franchise;
- a living artist, actor, composer, studio or director;
- a celebrity face or “blend”;
- a protected fictional location, vehicle, prop or costume.

Example:

> Full-body character design, optimistic neo-deco rescue fiction, asymmetrical cream conservator jacket, articulated glazed ceramic plates with visible amber repair seams, practical tool satchel, uncovered expressive face, stepped civic terraces and jade roofs after rain, bold clean ink shapes, warm reflected light, no cape, no mask, no chest emblem.

# Gameplay relationships

- **Luxwright + Cairnshift — Load Exchange:** Cairnshift moves a failing mass; Luxwright redirects the stress released by the swap.
- **Hushvector + Slipchord — Silent Service:** Dev removes disruptive vibration long enough for Ilyas to synchronise a route through a chaotic arena.
- **Vowglass + any ally — Witnessed Promise:** a voluntarily accepted mission constraint grants a strong team verb, but breaking it has a visible consequence.
- **Public trust:** rescue quality, truthful reporting and rebuilding choices affect powers because the lattice responds to collective confidence—not because popularity is a generic score.
- **Nemesis adaptation:** villains alter civic conditions and public arguments rather than merely equipping resistance to the player’s most-used damage type.

# Preliminary collision note

Initial broad web searches on 3 September 2026 found no obvious exact superhero/game results for **Luxwright, Hushvector, Cairnshift, Slipchord, Vowglass, The Quiet Sum, Morrowloan, Glass Parliament, Brass Orchard** or **Ledgerwraith**. That is encouraging but not clearance. Search engines are incomplete, rights can be unregistered, and trade marks are territorial and class-specific.

“Ravelin” and “Lumenward” were considered and rejected during ideation after searches surfaced existing entertainment uses. This document deliberately records that rejection as evidence of a clearance-aware process.

Before code migration, professionally search the final selected names and close variants in the relevant game, comic, entertainment, toy and clothing classes. Search civilian names and location names as well if they will be prominent in marketing.

# Migration recommendation

Do not bulk rename the old source yet. First:

1. approve or revise this creative direction;
2. select the final game title and city name;
3. run formal name clearance on the shortlist;
4. lock one canonical character sheet;
5. create original silhouette thumbnails without AI franchise/style references;
6. conduct a visual similarity review;
7. then map the approved canon into JSON IDs, TypeScript literals, save migrations, UI text and prompt documentation.

This order avoids spending development time replacing one uncleared name with another.
